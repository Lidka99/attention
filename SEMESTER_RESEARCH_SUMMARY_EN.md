During the reported period, research was conducted under the overarching dissertation topic *Deep Neural Models Compression with the Use of Structural Sparsification, Quantization and Entropy Coding Techniques*. The work comprised two separate but thematically related research streams. The main research stream concerned attention-based mechanisms for neural-network compression, while the second involved a separate compression method combining prototype-based filter approximation with a structurally sparse classifier. Both streams contributed to the structural-sparsification component of the dissertation and established a foundation for its future integration with quantization and entropy coding.

Within the main research stream, learnable attention-based gates were used to estimate the importance of filters and channels and to identify network components that could potentially be removed. Several approaches were investigated, including static channel selection, learnable channel gating, uncertainty-aware resource allocation, and semantic feedback. Experiments were conducted using ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 architectures on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet. The results provided evidence of considerable structural redundancy in deep neural models and clarified how attention-based importance estimates can support compression decisions. The research was subsequently extended towards adaptive-depth inference, in which selected computational blocks can be omitted for inputs that do not require the full network. This direction produced a promising practical outcome by maintaining competitive classification performance while reducing computational cost and inference latency.

The work also included the continued development of a rigorous and reproducible experimental framework incorporating controlled computational budgets, deterministic data splits, evaluations across multiple random seeds, statistical comparisons, class-level diagnostics, and direct measurements of computational cost and inference latency. Particular attention was paid to distinguishing soft gating, in which activations are only scaled or masked, from genuine structural sparsification or conditional execution that can produce measurable computational savings. These activities strengthened the structural-sparsification component of the dissertation and established a reliable basis for subsequent research. The next stages will focus on converting learned sparsity into compact, deployable architectures and integrating structural reduction with lower numerical precision and entropy-based weight coding.

The second research stream focused on completing and refining the camera-ready version of the article *Efficient Compression of Deep Neural Networks via Structural Sparsification*, which was accepted for presentation at the ISD 2026 conference in Prague. The article presents a unified compression pipeline combining prototype-based approximation of convolutional filters with a structurally sparse classifier and joint fine-tuning. The approach was evaluated using VGG-16-inspired models on the CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, demonstrating substantial reductions in model size and analytically estimated computational cost while maintaining competitive classification performance. In preparation for the conference, the presentation narrative, slides, visualizations, and detailed speaker notes were also developed and refined.
